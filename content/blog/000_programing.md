## I love everything

I wanted to become everything, or perhaps I should rephrase it with "I love making stuff".
I often gave up my vacations, touring/hanging out with friends or spending time with family.
Just me and my computer that obey me, with some books from library.
I split my day: morning for c/python for automation, afternoon for game and night for Android (java).
I can't exactly remember the order but I did split my time for each and everything that I wanted to do.
& what wanted often was programing.

Between class gaps, I often spent time in the computer lab and library. A few of my mates joined me in the lab & we helped each other understand different subjects. 
Once we had to have an argument with the IT guy because we few were keeping him waiting, and he turned off the internet :)
Yep, we went to the administrator & it was in our favor. The IT man never stood against us & we came to an understanding.

I even started attending Android (wireless) classes, which were 4–5 semesters ahead of my level.
Thanks to Jangir sir, who became my supervisor. So yes, I became that advanced in programming. 
Often I learned online, and classes were just for attendance marks, so I later skipped some.

### the cost

We have so little time but so many things we want to do... friends, family, and the desire to learn.
I ended up following my passion for playing with computers & programming.
I ended up becoming a night owl, sleeping less, and turning into a zombie in class.

Sometimes teachers asked me to wash my face so that it would remove my sleepy mood.
Later, sometimes I just asked teachers if I could take a 10–20 min nap in the middle of class.
Once a teacher found out about my interest, it became no issue. 
I skipped classes for tech events like GDG, yap, sometimes at kinda faraway places... 3–4h journeys.

I was even okay with just accepting average grades, which I couldn't care about because they weren't CSE-related.
I wasn't that good at math, but I could program something to solve things for me.

I might have became ended up becoming arrogant. The computer club wanted me to join,
but I skipped it because I found them less interested in programming and more interested in showing off themselves.
I didn't join any coding events... why, idk. I wasn't interested in being in a crowd and showing off, or was I afraid of failure?
Even during some university events, some companies wanted to hire me, but I still wanted to explore & learn... so I skipped.
